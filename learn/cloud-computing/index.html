Lesson 01 · Cloud concepts

☁️What cloud computing actually is

Cloud computing is renting compute, storage, and services over the internet — plus how you pay for it, and what serverless really means.

16 min read

You will understand

  • ✦A plain-English definition of cloud computing
  • ✦Why CapEx, OpEx, and consumption-based billing are different
  • ✦Pay-as-you-go vs reserved vs spot, at fundamentals level
  • ✦What serverless is — and what it is not

Why this shows up on AZ-900

AZ-900 wants you to contrast buying hardware (CapEx) with paying for what you use (consumption / OpEx), name the pricing models, and know that serverless still uses servers — you just do not manage them.

Official Microsoft Learn module

Describe cloud computing ↗

Units covered in this lesson:

  • What is cloud computing
  • Describe the consumption-based model

💡Cloud computing is not magic, and it is not "someone else's computer" as a joke. It is a business model: instead of buying servers, a building, cooling, and a night-shift operator, you rent computing over the internet and pay for what you use. Azure is Microsoft's version of that rental shop.

Picture electricity. You do not build a power plant behind your house. You plug in, the grid supplies capacity, and the bill tracks how much you used. Cloud computing does the same thing for virtual machines, storage, databases, networks, and software. Microsoft Learn's definition is: the delivery of computing services over the internet, typically with a pay-as-you-go price.

Think

Cloud = rent computing like you rent electricity. You do not own the plant.

What you are actually renting

Those "computing services" are not one product. They include servers (compute), disks and blobs (storage), databases, networking, software, analytics, and AI. You request them on demand, often in minutes, from a shared pool of resources that Microsoft already built and runs in datacenters around the world.

  • ▹On-demand: you can start a VM on a Tuesday afternoon without ordering hardware
  • ▹Shared pool: many customers share Microsoft's fleet, isolated from each other
  • ▹Measured: usage is metered — hours, gigabytes, transactions, executions
  • ▹Elastic: capacity can grow and shrink without you buying a new rack
  • ▹Self-service: a portal, CLI, or API, not a ticket to a datacenter team

CapEx, OpEx, and the consumption-based model

This is where AZ-900 starts scoring easy points. Companies spend money in two big buckets. Capital expenditure (CapEx) is a large upfront purchase of an asset — servers, racks, a building — that you depreciate over years. Operating expenditure (OpEx) is money you spend to run the business this month — electricity, salaries, a cloud bill.

CapEx

Buy the asset

  • • Big upfront spend: servers, datacenter, SAN, cooling
  • • You own it, whether it sits idle or not
  • • Slow to change — you guessed demand years ahead
  • • Classic on-premises model

OpEx

Pay to operate

  • • Ongoing costs: subscriptions, power, cloud invoices
  • • Usually easier on cash flow than a giant purchase
  • • Cloud bills are OpEx

Consumption-based

A style of OpEx

  • • You pay for what you actually used
  • • No (or very little) unused capacity sitting on the floor
  • • Stop the resource, stop most of the bill

Think

CapEx = buy the oven. OpEx = pay each month. Consumption = pay per pizza you actually bake.

Exam trap

Cloud is not automatically cheaper. It is more flexible. A VM you leave running 24/7 for three years can cost more than buying a box. The win is not running it when you do not need it — and not buying a warehouse of boxes "just in case."

Cloud pricing models you must be able to compare

"Consumption-based" is the idea. Azure then offers a few common ways to buy that capacity. At AZ-900 level you do not need rate-card math. You need to match a customer's situation to the right model.

Three models the exam loves to mix up

ModelThe dealBest whenWatch-out
Pay-as-you-goList price, billed for what you use, no termUnpredictable or short-lived work; experimentsHighest unit price if it runs all year
ReservedCommit for 1 or 3 years to a size/region (or similar commitment)Steady production that will run anywayYou still pay if you over-commit and stop using it
SpotUnused Azure capacity at a deep discountBatch, rendering, tests — work that can be interruptedAzure can evict the VM when it needs the capacity back

AZ-900 exam points

  • ★Pay-as-you-go = flexibility, no commitment
  • ★Reserved = discount in exchange for a time commitment
  • ★Spot = cheapest compute, but interruptible
  • ★Consumption-based is the umbrella idea: meter usage instead of buying hardware

Exam trap

Reserved is not the same as Spot. Reserved is a commitment you choose so Microsoft can plan capacity. Spot is leftover capacity Microsoft might take back. One is "I promise to stay." The other is "I will leave if you need the chair."

Serverless: you write code, the cloud runs it

Serverless does not mean there are no servers. It means you do not provision, patch, or scale those servers. You give Azure a piece of code (or a workflow), Azure hosts it, Azure scales it from zero to busy and back, and on a consumption plan you pay when it runs. Azure Functions is the poster child. Logic Apps is the workflow cousin.

Think

Serverless = you own the code. Azure owns the server, the OS, and the scale-out.

Exam trap

Serverless is not "free," and it is not IaaS. If the question says you must manage the operating system, that is not serverless. If it says you deploy a VM and install your app, that is not serverless either.

One sentence to remember

Cloud computing is renting metered IT like electricity: you trade CapEx for OpEx, pick pay-as-you-go, reserved, or spot depending on how committed and interruptible you are, and serverless is the extreme of "just run my code."

Exam-style scene · Exam-style scene

Fabrikam runs a nightly report that can be restarted if it fails. They want the lowest possible compute price and can tolerate the job being stopped mid-run. They do not want a one- or three-year commitment.

Pick: Spot (interruptible unused capacity)

Pay-as-you-go would work but costs more. Reserved is a commitment they do not want. Spot is designed for work that can be evicted. Serverless could also fit some jobs, but the exam cue "lowest price" plus "can be interrupted" is Spot.
