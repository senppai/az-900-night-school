🗂️The Azure resource hierarchy

Management groups, subscriptions, resource groups, and resources — who bills, who organizes, and where policy and RBAC hang.

15 min read

You will understand

  • ✦What a resource and a resource group actually are
  • ✦Why a subscription is the billing (and quota) boundary
  • ✦What management groups sit above subscriptions to do
  • ✦How RBAC and policy flow down the hierarchy
  • ✦Which Azure account types exist for getting started

Why this shows up on AZ-900

If a question mentions billing, think subscription. If it mentions a logical folder for resources you create, think resource group. If it mentions applying policy to many subscriptions at once, think management group.

📁Azure organizes everything in a tree. Once you can draw that tree from memory, a surprising number of AZ-900 questions are just “which level of the tree is this talking about?”

1. Resources

A resource is anything you create in Azure: a virtual machine, a disk, a virtual network, a storage account, an App Service plan. Each resource has a type, a name, and a region. You do not create “Azure” itself — you create resources inside a subscription.

Think

Resource = one thing you deploy. If you can see it in the portal and it has a billable meter, it is probably a resource.

2. Resource groups

A resource group (RG) is a logical container for resources. It is not a datacenter and it is not a network. You use it to group things that share a lifecycle — for example, all the pieces of “the HR app in production.” Delete the resource group, and Azure deletes the resources in it. A resource belongs to exactly one resource group. The resources inside one group can live in different regions, but the group itself has a location (that location stores metadata about the group).

AZ-900 exam point

  • ★One resource → one resource group (never two at once)
  • ★Deleting the group deletes the resources in it
  • ★Great place to apply tags, locks, and RBAC for a whole app
  • ★Not a billing boundary — the subscription is

3. Subscriptions

A subscription is the billing boundary. Microsoft sends the invoice for a subscription. It is also an access and quota boundary: each subscription has limits (how many vCPUs, how many storage accounts) and is tied to a Microsoft Entra tenant for identity. Companies often use several subscriptions to split bills (prod vs sandbox), to isolate environments, or to stay under quota limits. Creating a resource always happens in a subscription.

Exam trap

A resource group does not get its own invoice. The subscription does. Resource groups organize. Subscriptions bill.

4. Management groups

Management groups sit above subscriptions. They exist so you can apply Azure Policy and role-based access control (RBAC) to many subscriptions at once, instead of repeating yourself. Every Entra tenant has a root management group. You can nest management groups (think “Contoso / Europe / Production”) and hang subscriptions underneath. Management groups do not host VMs. They are an organization and governance layer.

Think

Management group = folder of subscriptions. Use it for policy and RBAC at scale, not for deploying a VM.

5. What each level is for

Billing lives at the subscription. Day-to-day organization and “delete this app in one click” lives at the resource group. The actual workload is the resource. Governance that must cover lots of subscriptions lives at the management group. RBAC and Azure Policy can be assigned at management group, subscription, resource group, or (for RBAC) even a single resource — and they inherit down the tree. That inheritance is why the hierarchy exists.

Pick the level that matches the job

LevelMain jobTypical exam hint
Management groupPolicy & RBAC across subscriptions“Apply to all subscriptions”
SubscriptionBilling, quotas, access boundary“Who pays?” / “invoice”
Resource groupOrganize resources, shared lifecycle“Delete together” / “logical container”
ResourceThe VM, disk, web app, VNet…“The thing you created”

Exam trap

You cannot put a subscription inside a resource group. The tree only goes management groups → subscriptions → resource groups → resources.

  1. 1. Management group

    "I need one policy or one set of admins across many subscriptions."

  2. 2. Subscription

    "This is the bill, the quota, and the hard isolation boundary."

  3. 3. Resource group

    "These resources belong together and should live and die together."

  4. 4. Resource

    "This is the actual VM, storage account, or virtual network."

Getting an Azure account

To create anything you need an Azure account, and that account gives you at least one subscription. Microsoft Learn names three ways to start: an Azure free account (free access to popular services for 12 months, an always-free tier of some services, and a credit to spend in the first 30 days), an Azure free student account (free services and a credit for 12 months, no credit card needed for verified students), and the Microsoft Learn sandbox (a temporary subscription created for you while you do a Learn exercise, cleaned up automatically).

  • ▹Azure account → owns one or more subscriptions
  • ▹Subscription → holds resource groups and is the billing boundary
  • ▹Management groups → up to six levels deep under the root, to organize subscriptions

Think

Account → subscription(s) → resource groups → resources. Management groups sit above subscriptions to organize them.

One sentence to remember

Resources are the things you create, resource groups organize them, subscriptions bill and cap them, and management groups apply policy and RBAC across many subscriptions.

Exam-style scene · Exam-style scene

A company has 40 Azure subscriptions. Security wants every subscription to require MFA for admins and to block creation of public storage accounts, without setting that 40 times.

Pick: Put the subscriptions under a management group and assign Azure Policy (and RBAC) there.

Management groups exist to govern many subscriptions at once. A resource group cannot contain subscriptions, and a single subscription policy would not cover the other 39.
